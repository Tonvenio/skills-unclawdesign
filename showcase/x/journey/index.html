Kiambu, Kenya, 1,800 m

Only the ripe cherries come down the hill.

Smallholder families in the hills north of Nairobi grow SL28 and SL34, and deliver their ripe cherries to a cooperative washing station.

The cooperative washing station

Washed in running water.

The fruit is stripped from the seeds and the beans are washed in channels of clean water. A washed coffee tastes clear: this one gives blackcurrant, grapefruit and cane sugar.

Huila, Colombia, 1,750 m

The second lot takes its time.

On a family farm in Huila, Caturra is honey processed: the skin comes off, the sticky fruit stays on, and the coffee dries slowly on raised beds under shade cloth for about two weeks. Expect red apple, panela and cocoa nib.

In transit

Two hillsides, one roasting day.

Both lots grow within a few degrees of the equator, at 1,800 and 1,750 metres. They travel separately and meet at the roastery for a single Monday roast.

Monday 12 October 2026

Batch 041: 18 kg, green to brown.

Both coffees are roasted on the same Monday. The Kiambu comes out at a light roast; the Huila goes a little further, to medium-light.

Ships by Wednesday 14 October

Made to fit through the letterbox.

Batch 041 ships within 48 hours of roasting, in a resealable valve bag that slips through the letterbox, so nobody has to wait in. Inside: notes on the farm that grew it, and a recipe.
