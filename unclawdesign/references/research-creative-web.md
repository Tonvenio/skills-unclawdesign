# Award-winning websites now behave like software

The creative web of 2024–2026 has moved past the page. The top prizes went to sites that behave like software. Igloo Inc, Awwwards Site of the Year 2024, is a real-time 3D ice world. The 2025 Site of the Year, for Lando Norris, is a WebGL athlete site delivered on Webflow. Messenger, Developer Site of the Year 2025, is a free multiplayer browser game. Craft-community case studies add about eighteen distinct archetypes, from scroll-rail 3D worlds and playable portfolios to interactive comics, configurators, generative gardens and launch sites for fictional products. The toolset behind them is unusually mature. CSS scroll-driven animations run natively in Chrome and Safari 26. Same-document View Transitions became Baseline in October 2025. GSAP and all its plugins became free in April 2025. Three.js r186 ships a WebGPU renderer that falls back to WebGL 2, and WebGPU now reaches about 87% of users. The gaps are specific, though. Firefox stable still lacks scroll-driven animations and cross-document transitions. WebGPU is not automatically faster. Scroll-scrubbed video only stays smooth when it is encoded with dense keyframes. The constraints are just as concrete. WCAG 2.2's Level A rule requires a pause control for any automatic motion lasting more than five seconds, whether or not the site honours reduced motion. Usability research shows that scroll-jacking disorients most users, and a canvas gives search engines and screen readers nothing. The production pattern that wins reconciles both sides: semantic HTML owns content, layout and the LCP element, and one visibility-gated canvas adds the experience. On that pattern the reduced-motion and low-GPU paths are cheaper versions of the site, not broken ones. For a generator like unclawdesign, distinctiveness no longer comes from adopting a technique, because OS-desktop, infinite-grid and chat-portfolio patterns are already sold as Framer templates. It comes from deriving one signature interaction from the subject itself and building it within a strict accessibility and performance budget.

## Juries crown software-like sites while templates commoditise novelty

The clearest signal of 2024–2026 is where the top prizes went. **Awwwards Site of the Year 2024 went to Igloo Inc**, a real-time icy 3D world by abeto, with sound by Bureaux. It is built on Three.js, custom shaders, Svelte and GSAP, plus a bespoke VDB-to-browser volume exporter, a procedural ice-crystal growth algorithm and in-browser live editing tools. Even its links section is a particle simulation: the particles swirl into a different shape for each external link and sync to the music ([Awwwards case study](https://awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html); [Awwwards SOTY 2024](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards-2024/site-of-the-year)). In 2025, **Site of the Year and Users' Choice went to OFF+BRAND's Lando Norris site**, tagged WebGL, GSAP and Webflow. **Developer Site of the Year went to abeto's Messenger**, a free, phone-friendly browser game in which visitors deliver mail on a tiny planet and meet other players who communicate only in emoji ([Awwwards Winners](https://www.awwwards.com/annual-awards/winners); [Awwwards site page](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lando-norris); [Aftermath](https://aftermath.site/messenger-browser-game-abeto)). E-commerce of the Year went to Locomotive's Scout Motors ([Locomotive](https://locomotive.ca/en/work/scout-motors)). abeto's two consecutive top-tier wins make it the defining studio of the cycle. Its profile sets the template for award-tier work: technical artists who build their own tooling and render content in real time instead of shipping pre-rendered video.

The Webbys reward a wider range. Best Use of Animation went to ToyFight's GSAP website (2024), OddCommon's "LMI: The Vincent van Gogh Experience" (2025) and OFF+BRAND's Aether 1 (2026). Best Visual Design – Function went to Cowboy, Bézier and FOLLOW.ART over the same years ([Webby animation](https://winners.webbyawards.com/winners/websites-and-mobile-sites/features-design/best-use-of-animation-or-motion-graphics); [Webby visual design](https://winners.webbyawards.com/winners/websites-and-mobile-sites/features-design/best-visual-design-function)). The 2026 Webbys also named Shopify's "Renaissance Edition" Best Home Page, and gave awards to WWF's "Blue Corridors" for data visualisation and DEPT's "Signs" for accessible tech ([Webby 2026](https://winners.webbyawards.com/winners/websites-and-mobile-sites/features-design/best-navigation-structure)). For editorial, civic and NGO clients, the Webbys are the more relevant benchmark, because they treat data stories, editorial features and accessibility as categories of craft.

Two structural shifts sit underneath the trophies. First, **the DOM-plus-canvas hybrid has become the default production pattern.** The 2025 Site of the Year, Aether 1 and ULTRAGRID's isometric-desk portfolio all deliver high-end WebGL through Webflow ([Awwwards site page](https://www.awwwards.com/sites/lando-norris); [Codrops: Aether 1](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/08/06/building-aether-1-sound-without-boundaries/); [Codrops case studies](https://tympanus.net/codrops/tag/case-study/)). In this hybrid the DOM handles CMS, SEO and layout, and a canvas layers on top. Second, owning a tool goes with sustained success. Studio Freight incubated the Lenis smooth-scroll library, Resn built its "Jelly" Web3D pipeline, Active Theory runs its internal "Hydra" framework, and Lusion describes building "its own system" for each project ([Codrops: Studio Freight](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/29/studio-freight-moving-missions-forward/); [LBB](https://lbb.lbbonline.com/news/resn-and-bader-rutter-win-awwwards-site-of-the-year-for-pioneer-corn-revolutionized); [Webby Crafted with Code](https://webbyawards.com/crafted-with-code/rick-and-morty); [Codrops: Lusion](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/04/13/lusion-where-digital-craft-meets-ambitious-experimentation/)). At the other end, novelty is sold off the shelf. Framer's marketplace lists the RetroOS and PixelOS operating-system portfolios, an "Infinite Drag Grid" component, and chat- and terminal-style portfolios ("Chatfolio", "CLIfolio"). Awwwards catalogues the same patterns as reusable inspiration elements ([Framer RetroOS](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/retroos); [Framer Infinite Drag Grid](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/components/infinite-drag-grid/); [Framer Chatfolio](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/chatfolio/); [Framer CLIfolio](https://www.framer.com/marketplace/templates/clifolio/); [Awwwards inspiration](https://www.awwwards.com/inspiration/retro-game-on-interactive-desktop-interface)). Once a pattern is a template, it no longer signals craft.

The failures repeat across archetypes: content that can't be found, poor performance on weak devices, a lost call to action, and accessibility. The strongest examples build the countermeasure into the concept. PHOTOYOSHI pairs its infinite gallery with a second viewing mode. Studio Freight rebuilt Psyop because its case studies were buried, and the fix was search, filtering and sharing. Garden Eight builds with a headless CMS and static generation, and optimises for loading speed, contrast and "AI-based information understanding" ([Codrops: Garden Eight](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=117849); [Codrops: Studio Freight](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2026/07/29/studio-freight-moving-missions-forward/)). Bruno Simon ships a lower-quality mobile preset, yet commenters still reported crashes and freezes, mostly in Chrome ([GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=24963)). Archetypes 10, 14 and 18 combine with the others: at the 2025–26 frontier, a 3D world carries an audio layer, a light presence layer and an AI layer, as in ryOS, Bruno Simon's folio and Messenger. Bruno Simon's rule for the presence layer is worth copying. He avoided shared physics because of server cost and abuse risk, and runs every visitor message through a moderation model ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)). The configurator is the only archetype with a measurable commerce link. Scout Design Studio prices **20,000+ combinations across 83 products** in real time, runs about 3,750 configuration sessions a month into a Shopify checkout, and uses the same tool on showroom iPads to send bills of materials to manufacturing. The case study gives no conversion figures ([Threekit](https://www.threekit.com/case-study-scout-design-studio)).

## Native CSS now carries decorative scroll motion; libraries still own the narrative

**CSS scroll-driven animations have run in Chrome and Edge since version 115 (2023) and in Safari since 26 (September 2025). Firefox stable still exposes them only in preview builds, so they are not Baseline.** The feature set covers `animation-timeline: scroll()`/`view()`, `animation-range`, named timelines and `timeline-scope` ([BCD animation-timeline](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/animation-timeline.json); [Chrome: scroll-driven animations](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations); [WebKit: Safari 26](https://webkit.org/blog/17333/webkit-features-in-safari-26-0/)). A secondary source claims Firefox 152 ships them behind a flag and that they are an Interop 2026 priority, but no primary Mozilla source confirms either claim ([buildmvpfast](https://www.buildmvpfast.com/blog/css-scroll-driven-animations-replace-js-2026)). These timelines plug into CSS Animations and the Web Animations API, so they can run off the main thread when they animate `transform` and `opacity`. In a Chrome case study, a JS scroll-linked animation turned janky under main-thread load while the CSS version stayed smooth ([Chrome case study](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/scroll-animation-performance-case-study)). Five pitfalls recur. The `animation` shorthand resets `animation-timeline`, so the timeline must be declared after it. `overflow: hidden` creates a scroll container that silently captures `scroll()` and `view()` lookups, so `overflow: clip` is the safe way to clip. View-timeline ranges are computed from the untransformed box. The default range animates for the element's whole time in view, which distracts unless `animation-range` narrows it. And Safari 26's `progress()` doesn't clamp ([Chrome docs](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/css-ui/scroll-driven-animations); [Shadeed: overflow: clip](https://ishadeed.com/article/overflow-clip/); [WebKit guide](https://webkit.org/blog/17101/a-guide-to-scroll-driven-animations-with-just-css/)). The flackr polyfill last shipped in May 2024 and only parses same-origin CSS ([flackr/scroll-timeline](https://github.com/flackr/scroll-timeline)). Chrome 146 adds scroll-*triggered* animations (`timeline-trigger`, `animation-trigger`), which replace IntersectionObserver for play-once reveals, but no other engine ships them ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/scroll-triggered-animations); [BCD animation-trigger](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/animation-trigger.json)).

**Same-document View Transitions became Baseline on 14 October 2025, when Firefox 144 shipped them.** Cross-document transitions via `@view-transition { navigation: auto; }` work in Chrome 126+ and Safari 18.2+ but not in Firefox ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/same-document-view-transitions-are-now-baseline-newly-available); [BCD @view-transition](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/at-rules/view-transition.json)). A multi-page site built with Astro, WordPress or plain HTML can therefore get app-like page transitions from a few lines of CSS, and Firefox users simply navigate normally. Several details belong in any implementation. Navigations must be same-origin, and Chrome drops the transition if the navigation takes longer than about 4 s. The `pageswap` and `pagereveal` events let a page assign `view-transition-name` just in time for a list-to-detail morph. `<link rel="expect" blocking="render">` holds the first render, so its effect on Core Web Vitals has to be measured. Duplicate names cause the whole transition to be skipped, while `view-transition-class` and `match-element` remove per-item naming boilerplate in all three engines ([Chrome: cross-document VT](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/web-platform/view-transitions/cross-document); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/View_Transition_API/Using)). For page transitions in Firefox, swup is maintained (4.10.0, September 2026), while Barba has not released since August 2024 ([npm swup](https://www.npmjs.com/package/swup); [npm @barba/core](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@barba/core)).

The platform matrix below uses MDN browser-compat-data, read on 11 October 2026 ([animation-timeline](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/animation-timeline.json); [view-transition-name](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/view-transition-name.json); [@property](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/at-rules/property.json); [text-box](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/text-box.json); [sibling-index](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/types/sibling-index.json); [interpolate-size](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/interpolate-size.json); [scroll-button](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/selectors/scroll-button.json)). `content-visibility` versions come from [web.dev](https://web.dev/articles/content-visibility).

**GSAP became free with version 3.13 on 29–30 April 2025, including SplitText, MorphSVG and every former bonus plugin.** The current release is 3.15.0 (13 April 2026) ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/); [npm gsap](https://www.npmjs.com/package/gsap)). It is free but not open source. Webflow's "Standard No-Charge License" allows commercial, client and SaaS use, and states explicitly that AI-generated code using GSAP is not a prohibited use. It does bar using GSAP in a no-code visual animation builder that competes with Webflow ([GSAP Standard License](https://gsap.com/community/standard-license/)). ScrollTrigger is still the most complete scroll-storytelling toolkit. It offers pinning with spacer math, scrubbing with catch-up seconds, label-based snapping, `containerAnimation` for horizontal sections inside vertical scroll, and `gsap.matchMedia()` for responsive and reduced-motion branches ([ScrollTrigger docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/ScrollTrigger/)). The rewritten SplitText is 50% smaller. It adds masks for lines, words or characters, plus `autoSplit`, which re-splits after fonts load or the width changes. It sets `aria-label` on the parent and `aria-hidden` on the fragments ([GSAP 3.13](https://gsap.com/blog/3-13/); [SplitText docs](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/Plugins/SplitText/)). For pointer-follow effects, the documented tool is `gsap.quickTo()` ([quickTo](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.quickTo()/)).

**Lenis (1.3.26, August 2026) is the de facto smooth-scroll layer because it keeps native scrolling.** Sticky positioning, anchor links and accessibility keep working, and it honours `prefers-reduced-motion` by default by forcing scroll to follow input 1:1. Its costs are main-thread work, a 60 fps cap in Safari (30 fps in low-power mode), no CSS scroll-snap, and smoothing that stalls over iframes ([Lenis README](https://github.com/darkroomengineering/lenis)). It earns its place when scroll must drive a WebGL scene. For content sites, native scroll plus sticky positioning and scroll-driven animations is lower risk.

The other libraries sort into active and stalled. **Motion** (MIT, 14.1.0 on 9 October 2026) is the React-first UI-motion choice, with a `scroll()` of about 5.1 kb. Its internals move fast, though: within weeks, scroll acceleration moved between ScrollTimeline, ViewTimeline and the main thread, so production sites should pin versions ([npm motion](https://www.npmjs.com/package/motion); [Motion changelog](https://github.com/motiondivision/motion/blob/main/CHANGELOG.md); [motion.dev scroll](https://motion.dev/docs/scroll)). **Anime.js v4** (MIT, 4.5.0) bundles a scroll observer, `splitText`, `scrambleText`, draggable and layout modules, which makes it the MIT-licensed counterpart to GSAP ([Anime.js docs](https://animejs.com/documentation/)). For designer-authored interactive vectors, **Rive** (state machines, data binding) and **dotLottie** (a Rust/WASM core with the ThorVG renderer, state machines, theming and worker rendering) are actively maintained, while classic lottie-web last shipped in May 2025 ([npm @rive-app/canvas](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@rive-app/canvas); [dotlottie-web](https://github.com/LottieFiles/dotlottie-web); [npm lottie-web](https://www.npmjs.com/package/lottie-web)). **Theatre.js** looks stalled, with its last release (0.7.2) in May 2024 and development moved "temporarily" to a private repo, and **Popmotion** has not released since August 2022 ([npm @theatre/core](https://www.npmjs.com/package/@theatre/core); [npm popmotion](https://www.npmjs.com/package/popmotion)).

Typography is the cheapest way to make motion look bespoke. Variable fonts let headlines animate weight, width, slant and optical size, and `@property` lets a registered `--wght` interpolate smoothly, including when the animation is linked to scroll. Setting `font-variation-settings` replaces every axis at once, so drive each axis through its own custom property ([MDN: Variable fonts](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/Guides/Fonts/Variable_fonts); [BCD @property](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/at-rules/property.json)). Changing an axis changes glyph metrics, so the text reflows every frame. Axis animation belongs on short display strings, never on body copy. `text-box` trim aligns large display type exactly to cap height, and `sibling-index()` makes pure-CSS staggers possible ([BCD text-box](https://github.com/mdn/browser-compat-data/blob/main/css/properties/text-box.json)). Pointer effects should tween transforms toward the cursor, run only under `(hover: hover) and (pointer: fine)` with no reduced-motion preference, and leave the native cursor visible ([quickTo](https://gsap.com/docs/v3/GSAP/gsap.quickTo()/)). Three effects tend to read as considered rather than generic: lighting that follows the pointer through CSS variables, tilt limited to the hovered component, and context labels such as "Drag" shown only over interactive media. That is design judgement, not a measured finding.

## WebGL 2 stays the floor as WebGPU reaches 87% of browsers

**Three.js r186 (24 September 2026) remains the default engine for creative sites.** Its `three/webgpu` renderer tries WebGPU in `init()` and falls back to WebGL 2, so a single TSL code path covers both. TSL lets developers write shaders in JavaScript and compiles them to WGSL or GLSL ([three.js releases](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases); [Utsubo migration guide](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)). The migration has a real cost. `ShaderMaterial`, `RawShaderMaterial`, `onBeforeCompile`, EffectComposer and pmndrs/postprocessing do not run on WebGPURenderer. In one author's tests, drei's troika-based `Text` and `@react-three/postprocessing` failed too. Post-processing moves to `RenderPipeline` with TSL display nodes ([Utsubo migration guide](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)). Release r184 added a NodeMaterial compatibility layer to the classic WebGLRenderer, which eases porting ([three.js releases](https://github.com/mrdoob/three.js/releases)).

**WebGPU now runs in Chrome and Edge, in Safari 26 on macOS and iOS, and in Firefox on Windows and macOS. That is about 87% of global users by caniuse's August 2026 figure.** Firefox on Linux and Android is not yet covered, and WebGPU is not automatically faster: some forum reports show slower scenes ([web.dev](https://web.dev/blog/webgpu-supported-major-browsers); [Utsubo migration guide](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide); [Utsubo 100 tips](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)). Commenters on Bruno Simon's WebGPU portfolio reported WebGPU disabled in Chrome on Linux and one frozen tab. These are anecdotes ([GeekNews](https://news.hada.io/topic?id=24963)).

Gaussian splats are emerging across all the major engines. PlayCanvas's SuperSplat editor streams them over WebGPU, Babylon.js 9 adds shadows for splats, and three.js r186 reportedly adds a native splat renderer, though the release notes fetched for this research don't mention it ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-06-09-web-game-engines-2026-comparison)).

|---|---|---|---|
| three.js WebGLRenderer + GLSL | core about 600 KB minified ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgl-three-js-site-seo-rankable-guide)) | Maximum compatibility; most Codrops- and Shadertoy-derived code | Heavy for a single effect |
| three.js WebGPURenderer + TSL | same package via `three/webgpu` | Compute work (GPGPU particles, fluids) with automatic WebGL 2 fallback | Custom shaders and post stack must be ported; not automatically faster ([Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgpu-threejs-migration-guide)) |
| OGL | 29 KB minzipped total ([oframe/ogl](https://github.com/oframe/ogl)) | Hero shaders, image distortion, single-effect pages | Fewer features; close to raw WebGL |
| Babylon.js 9 | full engine | Game-like scenes; built-in Havok physics, clustered lighting, free editor ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-06-09-web-game-engines-2026-comparison)) | Overkill for editorial scroll sites |
| PlayCanvas 2.2x | about 1–2 MB runtime | Editor-driven projects; splats via SuperSplat | Private editor projects are paid ([Cinevva](https://app.cinevva.com/blog/2026-06-09-web-game-engines-2026-comparison)) |
| PixiJS v8 | single `pixi.js` package | 2D at scale: 100k moving sprites at about 15 ms CPU per frame ([PixiJS](https://pixijs.com/blog/pixi-v8-launches)) | WebGPU doesn't beat WebGL in every case |
| p5.js 2.x | full library | Generative sketches; p5.strands JS-to-shader ([Processing Foundation](https://processingfoundation.org/blog/p5js-21-and-22-expanding-graphics-avenues-with-p5strands-improvements-and-webgpu)) | WebGPU renderer is experimental |

**Most award-winning shader effects reduce to three patterns.** The first is a textured quad for each DOM image with a distorting fragment shader, which covers hover ripples, RGB splits and displacement transitions. The second is a full-screen noise or raymarched pass. The third is a ping-pong simulation of fluid or particles that feeds a final composite with grain, bloom and chromatic aberration. Codrops' WebGPU tag lists 38 demos from November 2024 to September 2026, and they show the showcase moving toward TSL compute: water and fluid simulations, a GPU galaxy, flow-field particles, reaction-diffusion, shaped cursor trails, text destruction, page transitions and image relighting ([Codrops WebGPU demos](https://tympanus.net/codrops/demos/?tag=webgpu)).

Costs differ by orders of magnitude. The three.js manual notes that a city drawn entirely in a Shadertoy-style shader runs at **about 5 fps full-screen**, while a triangle-based game draws a similar city at 30–60 fps, because the shader runs for every pixel ([three.js manual: Shadertoy](https://threejs.org/manual/en/shadertoy.html)). GPU compute simulates 200,000 particles in a three.js example, while CPU-updated systems top out in the tens to low hundreds of thousands ([Utsubo 100 tips](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)). Post-processing has its own rules: disable multisampling when post-processing, tone-map last, merge compatible passes, and render bloom at reduced resolution. Half-resolution post can roughly double the frame rate in fill-bound scenes ([Utsubo 100 tips](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)).

The best-documented answer to the question of how to sync DOM and WebGL without lag is **HAOQI.DESIGN** (Codrops, August 2026). One fixed canvas renders every image, while transparent DOM placeholders positioned by CSS Grid own the layout, text and accessibility, and a `uRect` uniform maps screen UVs onto each card. R3F's frame loop drives Lenis, so scroll and render tick together, which removed a one-frame lag. Nearby cards are measured every frame and distant ones every 12 frames. An expensive star-flare pass runs at half resolution, every other frame, and only while visible, and under reduced motion the image-developing transition is skipped entirely ([Codrops: HAOQI.DESIGN](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=118395)). The author's main regret applies widely: next time he would set the mobile budget and each effect's shutoff conditions much earlier.

**Rapier (Rust compiled to WASM, about 1.4 MB) is the 2026 default for 3D physics.** Its SIMD npm builds are 2–5× faster than the fastest 2024 package. A new BVH broad-phase and a simplified friction model speed up contact-heavy scenes by about 25%, and v0.36 added soft bodies in September 2026 ([Dimforge 2025 review](https://dimforge.com/blog/2026/01/09/the-year-2025-in-dimforge); [Dimforge blog](https://dimforge.com/blog); [Cinevva physics](https://app.cinevva.com/tutorials/game-physics-libraries)). Matter.js (about 80 KB, fixed timestep since 0.20) is still the easiest way to make DOM letters or stickers fall and be flung while the text stays selectable. cannon-es struggles above about 100 bodies ([Cinevva physics](https://app.cinevva.com/tutorials/game-physics-libraries); [Matter.js release notes](https://code.fosterhangdaan.com/foster/liabru-matter-js/raw/branch/master/RELEASE.md)). Bruno Simon's 2025 folio shows the professional pipeline: physics bodies, colliders and respawn points are authored in Blender through naming conventions ([Awwwards case study](https://www.awwwards.com/brunos-portfolio-case-study.html)).

**Mobile 3D budgets are now well codified.** A scene should stay under about 100 draw calls and 100,000 vertices, cap the device pixel ratio at 2 (or 1–1.5 for full-screen shaders), and keep textures at 2048 px or less in KTX2, which stays compressed in GPU memory and uses 4–8× less of it; a single 4096² RGBA texture otherwise takes 64 MiB of VRAM. Geometry goes through Meshopt or Draco, dynamic lights stay at about three with 512–1024 px shadow maps, and GPU resources must be disposed of explicitly ([Utsubo 100 tips](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)). Award teams meet these budgets by faking expensive effects. Aether 1 runs its fluid simulation at **7.5% of screen resolution**, fakes depth of field, replaces bloom with a billboard, raycasts against invisible low-poly proxies and bakes ambient occlusion, and the team reports a steady 60 fps on a 2020 iPhone SE ([Codrops: Aether 1](https://tympanus.net/codrops/2025/08/06/building-aether-1-sound-without-boundaries/)). OFF+BRAND's Adrián Gubrica picks ogl for smaller bundles and replaces glass and depth-of-field pipelines with shader tricks and textures ([Codrops](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=105045)). Igloo Inc replaced a pre-rendered video intro with an in-engine one, compressed its volume data below the size of a typical image, and deferred shader compilation until after first load ([Igloo case study](https://awwwards.com/igloo-inc-case-study.html)). Device tiering with detect-gpu still works (tier 0 means no WebGL or under 15 fps). Its gfxbench data stopped updating in December 2025, though, so pair it with live frame-time monitoring such as R3F's `PerformanceMonitor` ([detect-gpu](https://github.com/pmndrs/detect-gpu); [Utsubo 100 tips](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)).

The asset pipeline runs from Blender, or Spline used as an asset tool that exports glTF, as HAOQI did to avoid runtime lock-in, through `gltf-transform optimize` (Meshopt, 2048 px textures) into three.js or R3F ([Codrops: HAOQI.DESIGN](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=118395); [Utsubo 100 tips](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/threejs-best-practices-100-tips)). **Shadertoy code is not free for commercial use by default.** Popular shaders, including Inigo Quilez's, carry CC BY-NC-SA 3.0, and community discussion treats that licence as the default when none is given ([three.js manual](https://threejs.org/manual/en/shadertoy.html); [VIDVOX forum](https://discourse.vidvox.net/t/user-bennoh-is-stealing-shaders-and-uploading-them-online-with-mit-license/2682)). Shadertoy's own terms page could not be checked. Reimplementing a technique is safer than pasting code.

## Keyframe density, posters and licences decide media-heavy pages

**Whether scroll-scrubbed video plays smoothly depends on keyframe density, not on the JavaScript.** Setting `currentTime` lands on the nearest keyframe, and default encodes place one every 2–10 s (x264's default interval is 250 frames). A 10-second clip with one keyframe per second therefore shows only about 10 distinct frames across the entire scroll ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)). The three viable methods trade bytes, smoothness and robustness differently:

| Method | How it works | Strength | Cost and failure modes |
|---|---|---|---|
| Canvas image sequence | Preload N frames and `drawImage` the frame at `floor(scrollFraction × frameCount)` inside rAF; the classic example uses 148 frames ([CSS-Tricks, 2020](https://css-tricks.com/lets-make-one-of-those-fancy-scrolling-animations-used-on-apple-product-pages/)) | Predictable everywhere; frames can be swapped per device and loaded progressively | One multi-sequence page made about 1,600 requests and moved about 56 MB; Apple serves a single still on slow connections |
| Seeked `<video>` | All-intra encode (`-g 1`) at the rendered size, `yuv420p`, audio stripped, WebM then MP4, `preload="auto"`, never `.play()` ([Hon Tran](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/scroll-scrubbed-video-stutters-fix)) | One resource with range requests | About 8 MB MP4 / 13 MB WebM at 1280 px vs 1–2 MB for a normal encode; iOS Low Power Mode breaks it with no workaround ([ScrollyVideo](https://www.npmjs.com/package/scrolly-video)) |
| WebCodecs to canvas | Demux, feed `VideoDecoder`, hold a ±1 s window of `VideoFrame`s and `close()` the rest ([Keng Lim](https://lionkeng.medium.com/a-tutorial-webcodecs-video-scroll-synchronization-8b251e1a1708)) | Smoothest scrubbing and reverse playback | Decode delay at load; manual GPU-memory management; Chromium-first, with iOS 26 support unverified |

The memory arithmetic explains the sliding window. A decoded 1080p RGBA frame is about 8.3 MB, so holding all 148 frames of a 1080p sequence would take about 1.2 GB. Every scrubbed sequence needs a still-image fallback, because Low Power Mode and data-saver settings defeat all of these methods.

Ambient video is simpler but has its own traps. Muted, inline video with no audio track autoplays in Chrome and iOS Safari. Unmuted autoplay needs prior engagement, so handle the `play()` promise and show a play button when it rejects ([Chrome autoplay policy](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay); [WebKit iOS video policies](https://webkit.org/blog/6784/new-video-policies-for-ios/)). **A video's LCP time is the poster load or the first frame, whichever comes first.** The poster, not the video, should therefore be the tiny, high-priority asset, and `<video loading="lazy">` now defers media below the fold ([web.dev: LCP](https://web.dev/articles/lcp); [web.dev: lazy-loading video](https://web.dev/articles/lazy-loading-video)). Stripping the audio track from a muted hero saves about 20% of its bytes ([MDN: video performance](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Learn/Performance/Video)). List sources with AV1 or VP9 WebM first and H.264 MP4 last. Safari plays AV1 only with hardware decode, on M3-or-later Macs, iPhone 15 Pro and iPhone 16 and later ([MDN: video codecs](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Formats/Video_codecs)). For long-form video, Chrome's rollout of native HLS (versions 141–142) produced playback errors that MSE-based players avoided, so keep an hls.js/MSE path ([Mux incident](https://status.mux.com/incidents/g4wcv5cm02sg)). Any loop longer than five seconds that runs next to other content needs a pause control under WCAG 2.2.2, and award-style sites often leave it out ([W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)).

AVIF and WebP are universally safe. **JPEG XL became usable in October 2026, when Chrome 155 began decoding it with the memory-safe `jxl-rs` on 6 October.** Safari has supported it since version 17, and Firefox has not shipped it ([Chrome blog](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/jpeg-xl-in-chrome); [MDN: image types](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/Media/Guides/Formats/Image_types)). In one test, a 990 KB photo compressed to 472 KB as JXL, against 507 KB as AVIF and 700 KB as WebP. Cloudinary measured JXL about 20% smaller than AVIF across 40,000+ images. AVIF still wins below about 0.4 bits per pixel and on graphics and transparency ([corewebvitals.io](https://www.corewebvitals.io/pagespeed/jpeg-xl-core-web-vitals-support)). JXL therefore belongs as an optional first `<source>` for large photographic heroes.

The LCP rules are blunt. Put the hero in the markup, not in a CSS background or JS. Never lazy-load it. Mark only one or two images `fetchpriority="high"`; that change alone cut Google Flights' LCP from 2.6 s to 1.9 s ([web.dev: optimize LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp); [web.dev: Fetch Priority](https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority)). Chromium excludes low-entropy blur placeholders from LCP, so a blur-up improves perceived speed without gaming the metric. A CSS-only LQIP packs a 20-bit placeholder into a single custom property with no JS ([web.dev: LCP](https://web.dev/articles/lcp); [Lean Rada](https://leanrada.com/notes/css-only-lqip/)).

**Procedural media is both the most distinctive option and the cleanest legally.** Remotion renders React components to video. It is free for companies of up to three employees, and company licences start at $25 per seat per month. Motion Canvas exports frame sequences. Mediabunny (MPL-2.0, with a 17 kB MP4 writer) encodes canvas frames to MP4 or WebM in the browser through WebCodecs ([Remotion licence](https://github.com/remotion-dev/remotion/blob/main/LICENSE.md); [remotion.pro](https://www.remotion.pro/license); [Motion Canvas](https://motioncanvas.io/docs/rendering); [Mediabunny](https://mediabunny.dev/)). Runtime shaders can replace stock video entirely, at the cost of GPU budget and a poster for LCP.

AI generation suits short ambient loops: Veo 3.x produces 4–8 s clips at 24 fps, always with audio and a SynthID watermark ([Gemini API: Veo](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/veo)). Commercial rights depend on the tool and plan. **Midjourney** requires companies with more than $1M in annual revenue to be on Pro or Mega to own their outputs, and makes content public by default ([Midjourney ToS](https://docs.midjourney.com/hc/en-us/articles/32083055291277-Terms-of-Service)). **FLUX [dev]** weights are non-commercial, though their outputs may be used commercially ([BFL licence](https://bfl.ai/legal/non-commercial-license-terms)). **OpenAI Sora** is gone: its app closed on 26 April 2026 and its API on 24 September 2026 ([The Decoder](https://the-decoder.com/openai-sets-two-stage-sora-shutdown-with-app-closing-april-2026-and-api-following-in-september/)). Prompts alone don't make an output copyrightable in the US, per the Copyright Office in January 2025. From 2 August 2026, Article 50 of the EU AI Act requires disclosure of realistic deepfake depictions, with a lighter notice for evidently artistic work ([USCO](https://copyright.gov/newsnet/2025/1060.html); [AI Act Art. 50](https://artificialintelligenceact.eu/article/50/)). Unsplash, Pexels and Coverr all allow free commercial use without attribution. Coverr bans using its footage for AI training or datasets, and it provides no releases for brands, landmarks or property ([Unsplash](https://unsplash.com/license); [Pexels](https://www.pexels.com/license/); [Coverr](https://coverr.co/license)).

## WCAG Level A and a decade of usability research fence in the motion

**WCAG 2.2 (W3C Recommendation, 12 December 2024) sets Level A limits that most experiential sites run into** ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). **SC 2.2.2 (pause, stop, hide)** applies to any motion that starts automatically, lasts more than five seconds and appears alongside other content. Content set off by scrolling into view counts as starting automatically, a control that pauses only while hovered or focused does not qualify, and W3C recommends a single page-wide control. Because 2.2.2 is a non-interference criterion, an accessible alternate version cannot excuse an unstoppable loop on the main page, and honouring `prefers-reduced-motion` alone does not meet it ([W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html)). **SC 2.3.1** caps flashing at three times per second, with a 341×256 px block approximating the area that matters, which makes glitch shaders a common hidden risk. **SC 1.4.2** requires a control for any auto-playing audio that lasts more than three seconds ([W3C 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)). **SC 2.5.7 (AA)** requires a single-pointer alternative to dragging. Browser-native scrolling is exempt, **but a page that suppresses or reimplements scrolling loses the exemption**, which directly implicates scroll-jacking and drag-to-pan canvases ([W3C 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html)). Sticky headers and cookie banners that cover the focused element fail **SC 2.4.11**, and the fix is `scroll-padding` ([W3C 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html)). The AAA criterion **SC 2.3.3** names parallax as the typical non-essential motion and is met through `prefers-reduced-motion` or a site-level toggle ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). The WCAG 3 Working Draft of 10 September 2026 lists "No visual motion" under its "Avoid physical harm" guideline, which suggests motion may become a lower-level requirement. That draft is still years from Recommendation ([WCAG 3 WD](https://www.w3.org/TR/wcag-3.0/)).

The population at stake is large. **In NHANES data from 2001–2004, 35.4% of US adults aged 40 and over, about 69 million people, had vestibular dysfunction** ([PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/19468085)). Triggers include large-area scaling and panning, parallax, zooming, surprise reveals, autoplaying video and animated gradients ([web.dev: prefers-reduced-motion](https://web.dev/articles/prefers-reduced-motion); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). `prefers-reduced-motion` has been Baseline since January 2020 and maps to OS settings on Windows, macOS, iOS, Android and the Linux desktops ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). The consensus pattern is "no-motion-first". Base styles stay static and motion is added only under `no-preference`. Spatial motion is *replaced* with dissolves rather than all feedback being deleted, smooth scrolling and autoplay switch off, and heavy animation libraries are never imported for users who asked for less motion. An on-site `role="switch"` toggle completes the pattern, because many users don't know the OS setting exists ([Smashing Magazine](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2021/10/respecting-users-motion-preferences/); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion)). Crossfades are the standard replacement because WCAG's definition of motion animation excludes opacity and colour changes ([W3C 2.3.3](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/animation-from-interactions.html)). `prefers-reduced-transparency` has limited support and `prefers-reduced-data` has none, so neither can carry essential behaviour ([MDN: reduced transparency](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-transparency); [MDN: reduced data](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-data)).

**Scroll-jacking disorients most users.** In NN/g testing in 2023, participants felt they had lost control: they scrolled frantically, grabbed the scrollbar, mistook long hijacks for bugs, and sometimes believed the page had ended. Task-oriented users left. The worst case combined an altered scroll rate or direction with text to read, and problems were worse on mobile. Sticky navigation as an escape route helped, as did short segments, a clear function (BBC's progressive disclosure), and placement below the fold; NN/g notes that 80% of viewing time is spent above the fold ([NN/g Scrolljacking 101](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/scrolljacking-101/)). Horizontal scrolling on desktop has a high interaction cost and is easily missed; in eyetracking, users ignored the arrows entirely ([NN/g, 2014](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/horizontal-scrolling/)). The only controlled parallax study (n = 86, 2015) found that parallax raised perceived fun (5.2 vs 4.7, p = .008). It made no difference to usability, satisfaction or visual appeal, and it made two participants motion-sick, one of whom couldn't continue ([Frederick et al., JUS 2015](https://uxpajournal.org/wp-content/uploads/sites/7/pdf/JUS_Frederick_Feb2015.pdf)). The evidence is thin and dated: the research found no large-sample study from 2024–2026, and NN/g's 2023 article gives no sample size. But it all points one way. Experiential motion buys delight, not usability, so it must never sit between a user and a task.

**Core Web Vitals thresholds are unchanged: LCP ≤ 2.5 s, INP ≤ 200 ms and CLS ≤ 0.1, measured at the 75th percentile, separately for mobile and desktop** ([web.dev: Web Vitals](https://web.dev/articles/vitals); [web.dev: INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp)). Animation-heavy sites fail in predictable ways. Large GSAP or Three.js bundles block the hero: Utsubo puts the Three.js core near 600 KB minified and full stacks past 3 MB, with each shader taking 50–300 ms to compile and each large texture 50–200 ms to upload. Scroll and pointer handlers starve the main thread. Entrance animations that reflow, unreserved canvases and font swaps shift the layout ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/core-web-vitals-for-animation-heavy-sites); [Utsubo SEO guide](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgl-three-js-site-seo-rankable-guide)). INP ignores scrolling, so scroll jank needs separate frame-rate checks ([web.dev: INP](https://web.dev/articles/inp)). The mitigations are well established. Compositor-only animation matters most: in web.dev's demo, animating `top`/`left` dropped about 50% of frames, against about 1% for `transform`. `content-visibility: auto` cut initial render from 232 ms to 30 ms in its demo while keeping content in the accessibility tree. 3D scenes should be imported dynamically, started only when visible, and moved into OffscreenCanvas workers where possible ([web.dev: animations](https://web.dev/articles/animations-guide); [web.dev: content-visibility](https://web.dev/articles/content-visibility); [Utsubo SEO guide](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgl-three-js-site-seo-rankable-guide)).

**A canvas contributes no indexable text, headings or links, and it is opaque to screen readers and to automated checkers.** So the H1, copy and CTA must be server-rendered HTML, meaningful 3D states need URLs, and interactive hotspots must be real focusable DOM elements ([Utsubo SEO guide](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgl-three-js-site-seo-rankable-guide)). Automated axe testing catches about 57% of issues by volume, and none inside a canvas ([Deque](https://www.deque.com/blog/automated-testing-study-identifies-57-percent-of-digital-accessibility-issues)). Manual passes should cover the dominant screen-reader pairings: JAWS or NVDA with Chrome on desktop, and VoiceOver on iOS, which 70.6% of respondents use on mobile ([WebAIM Survey #10](https://webaim.org/projects/screenreadersurvey10/)). Chrome DevTools can emulate reduced motion, reduced transparency, contrast preferences and forced colours ([Chrome DevTools](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/devtools/rendering/emulate-css)).

## A three-tier playbook: accessible floor, one signature, opt-in frontier

The research converges on a four-layer architecture. **L0** is semantic HTML that works without JS. **L1** adds CSS layout, typography and motion, gated behind `no-preference` and `@supports`. **L2** is JS enhancement loaded at idle and skipped under reduced motion, Save-Data or weak hardware. **L3** is a visibility-gated WebGL or WebGPU layer with a static fallback. The rules below are ordered so that an agent lays the floor first, spends its novelty budget once, and treats the frontier as opt-in. Mapped onto unclawdesign's job types, **conversion, docs, dashboard and product-UI jobs** keep a conventional structure with tier-1 craft (native CSS motion, typographic reveals, at most image shaders). **Brand, portfolio and event jobs** may adopt one experiential archetype from the catalogue. **Editorial jobs** map to scrollytelling or magazine archetypes, and **ecommerce jobs** to the configurator or the scroll-scrubbed product reveal.

| Tier | Rule | How to apply it | Basis |
|---|---|---|---|
| P0 | Content lives in HTML; the canvas is a layer | Server-render the H1, copy, links and CTA; mark decorative canvases `aria-hidden="true"`; mirror any WebGL text in the DOM; give meaningful 3D or configurator states URLs and real focusable hotspot buttons | [Utsubo](https://www.utsubo.com/blog/webgl-three-js-site-seo-rankable-guide); [HAOQI](https://tympanus.net/codrops/?p=118395) |
| P0 | The LCP element is HTML, never a WebGL frame, preloader or video | Hero headline or a small AVIF/WebP poster in markup with `fetchpriority="high"`, never lazy; start 3D and video after load or on visibility | [web.dev LCP](https://web.dev/articles/optimize-lcp); [Fetch Priority](https://web.dev/articles/fetch-priority) |
| P0 | Write motion no-motion-first and decide before importing | Static base CSS; motion only inside `prefers-reduced-motion: no-preference`; check `matchMedia` before importing GSAP, Lenis or three.js; under `reduce`, crossfades replace slides and zooms, and posters replace loops | [Smashing](https://www.smashingmagazine.com/2021/10/respecting-users-motion-preferences/); [MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/CSS/@media/prefers-reduced-motion) |
| P0 | One page-level motion switch for anything moving longer than 5 s | A `role="switch"` early in tab order that stops WebGL loops, video and marquees, persists the choice, and never depends on hover or focus | [W3C 2.2.2](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/pause-stop-hide.html) |
| P0 | Hard ceilings on flashing and sound | No more than 3 flashes per second (audit glitch and strobe shaders); sound strictly opt-in; any audio over 3 s gets its own control; audio-reactive sites must work muted | [W3C 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html); [WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/); [Chrome autoplay](https://developer.chrome.com/blog/autoplay) |
| P0 | Keyboard and single-pointer parity | Buttons for every drag, orbit and pan; custom scrolling keeps Space, PgUp/PgDn and Home/End working; targets at least 24×24 px; `scroll-padding` under sticky headers | [W3C 2.5.7](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/dragging-movements.html); [W3C 2.4.11](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/focus-not-obscured-minimum.html) |

if (!calm) {
  const { getGPUTier } = await import('@pmndrs/detect-gpu');
  const { tier } = await getGPUTier();
  if (tier > 0) {
    const { start } = await import('./scene.js');               // code-split 3D
    const fx = await start({ dpr: Math.min(devicePixelRatio, tier > 2 ? 2 : 1.5) }).catch(() => null);

      .observe(document.querySelector('#fx'));

  }

Before shipping, every generated site should clear a release gate. Field data at p75 must pass all three vitals on mobile. In the lab, Lighthouse mobile should show no task over 50 ms during hero load, and a pass through every pinned section under 4× CPU throttling should hold its frame rate ([hontran.dev](https://www.hontran.dev/blog/core-web-vitals-for-animation-heavy-sites)). Reduced-motion emulation should show no parallax, smooth scrolling, autoplay or heavy library download, and the site should fall back cleanly with WebGL disabled and under forced context loss. A keyboard-only pass and screen-reader passes with NVDA or JAWS on Chrome and VoiceOver on iOS come next, then Harding FPA or PEAT analysis for any strobe or glitch effect ([W3C 2.3.1](https://www.w3.org/WAI/WCAG22/Understanding/three-flashes-or-below-threshold.html)). Finally, the layout must work at 320 px, with horizontal sections collapsing to vertical stacks ([WCAG 2.2](https://www.w3.org/TR/WCAG22/)).

## Conclusion

The bottleneck has moved from capability to fit. Native CSS timelines, Baseline View Transitions and a free GSAP have made craft-tier motion almost free. Framer templates turned the novel archetypes of a few years ago into commodities. The award winners all built their own system: abeto's volume exporters, Lusion's system for each project, Aether 1's faked optics that hold 60 fps on an iPhone SE. That suggests what is scarce now is a metaphor drawn from the subject itself, plus the discipline to fake an expensive look within budget. For an AI generator the risk is specific. If it reaches for the same "experiential" move every time, it recreates the template problem at machine speed. The novelty budget should be spent once per site, on a signature system derived from the brief, and everything around it kept deliberately restrained.

The constraints line up with each other more than the "creative versus accessible" framing suggests. One architecture, semantic HTML first with a gated canvas second, satisfies search and AI-crawler understanding, WCAG's non-interference rule, LCP and graceful fallback all at once. Accessibility becomes a structural choice rather than a tax added at the end. Three things remain unknown. There is no field data on conversion or SEO for experiential archetypes. The usability evidence is a decade old or lacks sample sizes. The dates for Firefox shipping scroll-driven animations and cross-document transitions are unconfirmed, and so is the conformance level WCAG 3 will give motion. When Firefox ships scroll-driven animations, the last reason to use JS scroll libraries for decorative effects goes away. Gaussian splats and WebGPU compute are the next frontier for cheap photoreal 3D and for real-time fluids and particles. Support tables should be re-checked against browser-compat-data before any claim is hard-coded into the skill.
